Commits to

  • One object above the fold: a book you can see is a book (Kimi: teal cover beside its open page; Astra: a spread with cover left, passage right).
  • "Read a book at your level, then talk about it" as the whole promise, one sentence under it.
  • Four ways as numbered prose rows with the tutor explained first, in plain words.

Trades away

  • The book is a still. It shows a page, not what happens when you tap, listen, or speak; the act lives in the text below.
  • Kimi's cover brings a second hue (teal) into the hero; allowed for cover art only, but it competes with the CTA for the eye.
  • Both variants fail contrast on 11px mono labels in ink-3 (2.6:1); fixable, the token is wrong at that size. Kimi's header nav clips at 375.

Right when

You want the shared-link visitor to get "a book, free, at my level" before anything else, and you are fine explaining the tutor in words rather than showing it.

Taken all the way

The book becomes the only image on the page: real cover art per language, the passage typeset on its page, the screenshots demoted to captions under the fold.

Direction B · field guide, rail and column · the structure

The Rail

This direction says: this page is the reader. A margin rail holds the name, the promise, and the one button while the column opens straight into the sample passage as chapter one, then the tutor as chapter two, then the four ways, then install.

Commits to

  • A persistent margin: wordmark, promise, Start free, sample and sign-in links never scroll away on desktop (Astra keeps a small cover there too).
  • Chapters, not sections: 01 the passage, 02 the tutor, 03 install or the four ways, so the page reads like the product it describes.
  • Astra's headline "Read it. Then talk about it." is the clearest value line of the four mockups.

Trades away

  • At 375 the rail collapses to a header and the structure's whole point disappears; the phone visitor gets a long single column.
  • Astra's page is 4,450px tall at 1280: a lot of scroll before install and the four ways.
  • This is a field guide again (V4's register) with a better rail; the repeat has to be justified by fit, not habit.

Right when

Most first visits come from a laptop, and you want the promise and the button in view for the whole read while the column does the showing.

Taken all the way

The rail gains the reader's own chrome: chapter label, level, a progress hairline that fills as the visitor scrolls, so the page literally behaves like a Sotto book.

Where the tutor runs

Below the stage

Two real screenshots as a captioned pair, then install: the book closes onto a phone home screen with only your device's steps beside it, then the footer. Nothing else. The four ways never get a section of their own; they are the Power scene's footnote.

The footnote is the pricing page

Select a source above. Each one rewrites one sentence, in the same voice, with the exact facts: nothing stored on the plan beyond usage minutes and billing; the key stays on the device; self-hosting is free and one command.

On the real page

The book is sticky at 1280 and the five scenes advance by scroll, 240ms per state change, no looping. At 375 the book stacks above the scene copy and the chips stay as tabs. Under reduced motion, every scene is a tab.

Commits to

  • Show, not tell: the tutor is explained by a two-line exchange on the page, before anyone reads a paragraph about it.
  • Power as a footnote under the act. Complaint 3 and 4 are answered by one radio row and one sentence each.
  • One object, one device, the app's own tokens: peach cutout, 18% peach tap fill, marker stroke, speech fill, accent ring.

Trades away

  • It is the most build: sticky stage, scroll-driven scenes, a reduced-motion tab fallback, and a device-detected install block. Two rounds, not one.
  • The passage is the only foreign-language prose on the page, so the stage carries the whole hero; if it reads as a demo widget rather than a book, the direction fails.
  • Screenshots move below the fold, so the "real, not stock" proof arrives second.

Right when

You believe the value is the loop (read, tap, listen, speak) and not the catalog, and you want the visitor to feel a tap and a reply within the first screen.

Taken all the way

The stage is live: the tap actually glosses, the play ring actually narrates the sentence with the app's speech fill, and Speak plays a six-second recorded exchange with captions.
