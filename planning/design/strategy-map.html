Sotto · planning/design/strategy-map · 2026-09-05 · advisor sheet, not a pitch

Five routes to a product, one worth taking first.

Noel's read: free to self-host, maybe a domain; pay if you want OpenAI without hosting anything, billed through Stripe; App Store optional. This sheet agrees with that read and adds two stops he has not priced yet: bring-your-own-key, which gives most people the paid experience for free, and a deploy kit, which is what "free to deploy" actually requires. Costs are from the measured logs; anything inferred is tagged.

What each route costs, who pays it, and what Noel owes.

A · Now

Free, self-host

This route says: Sotto is a thing you can run, not a thing you subscribe to. Reading, narration, tap-translate, vocabulary, the local and in-browser tutors, and import on your own machine.

live at sotto-steel.vercel.app
User pays
$0 plus their own hardware for local models.
Noel pays
$0 fixed · Vercel Hobby Domain is the only spend worth making now: about $12/yr.
Noel owes
A README a stranger can follow in 30 minutes. Content QA on the FR/ES drafts. Nothing on-call.
Right when
Always. This is the floor every other route stands on (CONTRACTS §0: useful with no cloud).

The tutor on a phone. The in-browser tutor needs desktop WebGPU; the local tutor needs a server the phone can reach.

B · Next, no server

Bring your own key

This route says: if you already pay OpenAI, you get the paid experience for free. The user pastes their key once; the static PWA talks to OpenAI directly for the tutor cascade and import glossing. Works on the phone.

User pays
~$0.012/tutor-min at OpenAI's rates, measured. A heavy learner spends $3 to $8 a month, straight to OpenAI.
Noel pays
$0 No accounts, no broker, no key of his in play.
Noel owes
A settings field, honest copy about where the key lives (on the device only), and the browser-side cascade against api.openai.com. inferred OpenAI allows browser CORS; the Realtime WebSocket also accepts a standard key from a page. Both need a 20-minute proof.
Right when
Immediately after A. It is the cheapest way to give Noel himself OpenAI voices on the iPhone, and it does not break CONTRACTS §0 (a key is neither auth nor payment).
Trades away
It cannibalizes D: anyone technical enough to get a key will never subscribe. That is fine. It also makes D honest: D sells convenience, not access.
C · When the README is true

Deploy kit

This route says: "free to deploy" is a promise with a command attached. One docker-compose or fly.toml that brings up apps/server plus the static client on one origin, with the deployer's key. That is also a personal hosted Sotto for Noel.

User pays
~$5/mo for a small machine, plus their OpenAI usage. Or $0 on a home box behind Tailscale.
Noel pays
~$5/mo if he runs his own instance. Otherwise nothing.
Noel owes
The template, a docs page, and keeping it green in CI. inferred serverUrl() already resolves to the page origin off-localhost, so same-origin deploys need no client change.
Right when
After B, before D. If D ever launches, this is the same server shape minus accounts, so the work is not wasted.
Trades away
Support questions from people whose deploy failed. Keep the kit to one provider and say so.
D · On trigger

Paid hosted, Stripe

This route says: pay $10 and never think about keys, servers, or minutes. One plan. The scaffolding exists in sotto-cloud and sits parked until three strangers ask, or Noel's own bill on C tops $10 a month, or someone offers to pay.

User pays
$9.99/mo or $79/yr 250 tutor minutes on the OpenAI cascade, 2 hosted imports with lazy narration.
Noel pays
~$26/mo fixed Fly machine ~$5, Vercel Pro $20 (Hobby is non-commercial, inferred), domain. Plus vendor cost: ~$1.70 per typical subscriber, $5.00 at every cap.
Noel owes
Accounts, support, uptime, a privacy policy and terms, refunds, and sales tax. Stripe Tax or a merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy at roughly 5%) handles VAT for you; plain Stripe does not. Six HIGH findings in the money path get fixed first.
Right when
Someone who is not Noel wants the tutor and will not paste a key. Break-even is about five web subscribers before Noel's time.
Trades away
Quiet. This is the first route with an on-call surface and a monthly bill that arrives whether anyone pays or not.
E · Spur

App Store

This route says: be found, be trusted, be installed in one tap. The PWA already installs from Safari, but almost nobody knows Share, then Add to Home Screen. The store fixes that and charges for it.

User pays
same $9.99 or a higher SKU if Apple takes 30%.
Noel pays
$99/yr + 15 to 30% 15% needs Small Business Program enrollment. Worst-case vendor share climbs from 53% to 59% or 72% of net.
Noel owes
Sign in with Apple, in-app account deletion, review with a demo account, privacy labels, an Expo account, StoreKit. Every one is a standing obligation. The Expo build already prebuilds cleanly, so the option is free to keep.
Right when
D has paying web users and the support inbox says "how do I get it on my phone." Also worth knowing: inferred Sotto may qualify as a "reader" app, which lets it link out for sign-up instead of using IAP. Check before building StoreKit.
Trades away
Margin and calendar. Two real upsides the PWA cannot match today: install trust, and reliable background narration with lock-screen controls on iOS (needs a test on a real phone).

Advisor's read

Four things I would push on.

Who is the customer of D?

Someone who found Sotto, wants the tutor, and will not paste a key. After B exists, that is a language learner, not a developer. Price convenience for them, and expect them to arrive through a friend or a blog post, not GitHub.

B before D is the whole argument

Bring-your-own-key gives Noel the phone tutor this month for the cost of a settings field. If, after four weeks, his own OpenAI bill is under $10, the paid tier has no customer yet, including him.

The domain is a yes, the brand is a question

Buy the domain now; it is the cheapest thing on this sheet and it makes the README real. The pending "S SOTTO" mark is a reason to keep the name provisional until D is real.

Curiosity about the store is fine, spend nothing on it

The Expo build already prebuilds. Keep it buildable, test background narration on a real iPhone once, and open the store question only when the support inbox does.
